# Slide skeleton — fillable, built for speed not beauty

Per your event's submission guidelines, the deck should cover: problem,
approach, techniques, challenges. Here's a slide-by-slide skeleton — aim
for 6-8 slides total, since your actual scoring moment is the live demo,
not the deck.

## Fast-build method

Don't hand-design this. Options, fastest first:
1. Paste this skeleton into Claude/an AI tool and ask it to draft the
   actual slide copy from your triage worksheet + demo script — you'll
   have real content in minutes.
2. Use a template tool (Gamma, Canva, PowerPoint's Designer) and paste
   in the content below — let the tool handle layout/design entirely.
3. Google Slides/PowerPoint from scratch, ONE visual per slide max,
   large text — a jury reads slides for 10 seconds each, not more.

## Slide-by-slide content

**Slide 1 — Title**
Team name, problem statement title, event name/date.

**Slide 2 — The problem**
1-2 sentences from your problem statement. One supporting stat or
scenario if the problem statement provides one. No solution talk yet.

**Slide 3 — Our approach**
The 5-agent pipeline as a simple left-to-right diagram: [Detection] →
[Risk] → [Retrieval] → [Recommendation] → [Escalation]. This single
slide is your strongest visual — it's the thing that shows "multi-agent"
isn't just a buzzword in your pitch.

**Slide 4 — Architecture**
One diagram: data → agents → API → dashboard. Keep it to 4-5 boxes,
don't cram the full file structure in.

**Slide 5 — Tech stack**
Logos/names of what you used (from `tech_stack_cheat_sheet.md`), grouped
as Backend / AI / Frontend.

**Slide 6 — Live demo**
Just a slide that says "Live Demo" — this is your cue to switch to the
actual application, not a slide with content.

**Slide 7 — Results**
The 2-3 numbers from your "Results & metrics" demo segment, large font,
one per line.

**Slide 8 — Challenges & what's next**
1-2 real challenges you hit (honesty reads well — e.g. "provider
switching for offline development," "grounding recommendations without
hallucination"). Then 2-3 concrete next steps.

## What NOT to do

- Don't put your live demo INSIDE the slides as embedded video unless
  you have zero confidence in live demo working — a live demo scores
  better if it works, per most rubrics rewarding "working application"
- Don't write paragraphs — bullet fragments only, you're narrating over
  it live
- Don't spend more than 30 minutes total on deck design — content over
  polish, your time is better spent rehearsing the live demo
